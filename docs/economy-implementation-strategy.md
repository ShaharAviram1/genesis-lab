## Findings Accepted

The following conclusions from `economic-progression-analysis.md` are treated as decided:

1. Energy income is 50–100× faster than reaction energy demand. Energy is not a meaningful resource.
2. The dominant wall is time (reaction duration) and queue (single slot), not economy.
3. Reactant quantities are all 1. Quantity scarcity does not exist.
4. Prestige pressure requires economic walls to exist before Big Bang feels like a decision.
5. Gen 5–6 cannot be designed until the economy supports them.

## Part 2 — Scarcity Architecture

### How Scarcity Should Be Represented

The game has three usable scarcity dimensions available without new systems:

| Scarcity type | Mechanism | Current state | Phase Q target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Energy scarcity** | Energy income vs. reaction energy cost | Ratio 50–100:1. No wall. | Ratio 5–10:1 at mid-game. Meaningful but not punishing. |
| **Quantity scarcity** | Reactant quantities > 1 | All quantities are 1. No accumulation friction. | Gen 2+ requires 2–5 units of intermediates. Gen 3–4 requires 5–15. |
| **Time scarcity** | Reaction duration in seconds | Gen 4 capstone = 900s. Exists. | Mostly preserved. Minor adjustments only. |
| **New currency scarcity** | A resource that cannot be generated by clicking | Does not exist. | Phase R / Gen 5 gate. Not Phase Q. |

**Energy scarcity and quantity scarcity are the two levers for Phase Q.**

Time scarcity already exists and is already the dominant wall. Phase Q does not primarily address it — that is prestige's job (time reduction blueprints in Phase R). Do not conflate the two problems.

---

### Evaluation: Which Scarcity Source First

**First: Quantity scarcity** — implement before energy rebalancing.

Reason: quantity changes are the safest to tune. They affect only seed data, not constants. A wrong quantity choice is easy to identify (too slow = reduce quantity, too fast = increase quantity) and does not cascade into the energy system. Quantity scarcity also creates the most natural economic walls: if Stainless Steel requires Steel×3 instead of Steel×1, players must queue and wait for steel production before queuing stainless. This is economic pressure without touching energy income.

**Second: Energy rebalancing** — implement after quantity changes are validated.

Reason: changing `ENERGY_MULTIPLIER` or `CLICK_ACTIVITY_GAIN` affects the entire session feel, not just a single reaction. It is a global constant, not per-reaction tuning. If quantity changes alone create sufficient pressure (the hypothesis), energy rebalancing may be minimal. If quantity changes are not enough, energy rebalancing is the second tool.

**Third: Shard formula recalibration** — implement after both quantity and energy changes.

Reason: the formula must be validated against actual production rates. If Gen 3 intermediate production volume increases (because more units are needed per chain), `log₂(produced+1)` terms will shift. Recalibrate last, with real playtest data or simulation.

**Not in Phase Q: new currency scarcity.** This belongs to Phase R (automation production as a new input) and Gen 5 design (prestige-gated reaction inputs). Introducing a new currency in Phase Q would couple the economy overhaul to prestige infrastructure that is not yet built.

## Part 3 — Intrinsic Value Strategy

**Recommendation: Option D — IV as a design tool only. Never expose in code.**

### Why Not the Other Options

**Option A (calculate dynamically):** Would require a recursive traversal of the reaction graph at runtime for every substance query. This adds non-trivial complexity to routes or queries for a metric that provides no gameplay value to the player. The player does not need to see IV.

**Option B (store IV in seeds):** Seeds would contain a derived field that must be kept manually consistent with `energyCost` and `reactant` values. A seed author who changes one reaction's `energyCost` must then recalculate every downstream substance's IV. This is fragile and prone to drift.

**Option C (generate during seed validation):** Better than B, but still couples a derived metric to the seed execution pipeline. Adds complexity to the seed runner for a value that has no gameplay function.

**Option D (design tool only):** IV is a spreadsheet or script calculation used during seed authoring to verify that proposed balance changes are internally consistent — that Gen 3 substances represent 4–6× the economic depth of Gen 2, that no anomalies exist like Steel's IV=35 surprising the designer. It is not stored in Mongo, not queried by routes, not displayed to players.

### How to Use IV in Phase Q

Maintain an IV audit script (or spreadsheet) that takes the reaction seed data as input and outputs IV per substance. Run it before and after any seed change to verify:

1. Generation averages maintained at ~5–6× growth per tier
2. No unintended anomalies (a Gen 3 substance with lower IV than Gen 2 average)
3. Proposed quantity changes do not collapse the spread within a generation

The script should be a standalone Node script in `/server/dev-*` or similar, not part of production code. It reads `seedReactions.js` and `seedSubstances.js` as source of truth and prints a report.

## Part 4 — Quantity Scaling Strategy

### The Problem

All reactant quantities are currently 1. This means:
- Every reaction requires exactly one of each intermediate
- Players synthesize intermediates on demand, one at a time
- No inventory accumulation is needed
- No planning is required

### The Goal

Quantity requirements should create **accumulation friction** — the experience of needing to build a stock of an intermediate before the next synthesis can proceed. This is economic pressure without requiring new systems.

### Implementation Framework

**Step 1: Identify bottleneck substances per generation.**

Not all substances need quantity > 1. Target the substances that appear as reactants in multiple downstream recipes — the ones that would naturally become stockpile targets. These are the economic chokepoints.

Examples of natural stockpile targets (structural, not final values):
- Gen 2: Iron Oxide (feeds copper, tin, nickel, chrome, silicon) — increasing Iron Oxide quantity pressure creates upstream iron/oxygen demand
- Gen 3: Graphene (feeds Carbon Nanotube and Lithium Ion Cell) — increasing Graphene quantity makes the two most important Gen 3 products more expensive simultaneously

**Step 2: Define quantity per generation tier.**

Suggested framework (structure only, not final values):

| Generation | Intermediate quantities | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Gen 1 | 1–2 | Early game should not punish. Small quantity signals that accumulation matters. |
| Gen 2 | 1–3 | Materials and foundry work. 2–3 of common intermediates. |
| Gen 3 | 2–5 | Advanced materials. Steel×2 for Stainless Steel, Graphene×2 for CNT — this already exists and is correct. Add 3–5 to other slots. |
| Gen 4 | 3–10 | Capstone synthesis. Should require meaningful stocks. Hydrogen Plasma used in 3 reactions — raising its demand creates a real queue planning problem. |

**Step 3: Apply changes per-reaction, not per-substance.**

The `quantity` field lives on the `reactants` array in `seedReactions.js`, not on substances. Update it reaction by reaction. Each reaction entry specifies:
```js
reactants: [
    { key: 'graphene', quantity: 2 },  // was 1
    { key: 'carbon',   quantity: 1 }
]
```

No schema changes required. The `quantity` field already exists in `Reaction.reactants`.

**Step 4: Check the `checkReactionEligibility` logic.**

The route that validates whether a player has enough inventory to queue a reaction uses these quantities. No code change expected — it already compares `user.inventory[substance].quantity >= required.quantity`. Verify this holds after seed changes.

**Step 5: Validate inventory impact on existing users.**

Existing users who have 1 of an intermediate in inventory will not be blocked immediately — they will only feel the new requirement when they attempt to queue a reaction that now needs more. This is acceptable migration behavior (see Part 5).

## Part 5 — Migration Strategy

### The Problem

Four generations of content exist with players who may have in-progress saves. An economy overhaul that changes all quantities, energy costs, and reaction times simultaneously would:
- Potentially strand saves mid-synthesis
- Make in-progress queue entries resolve incorrectly (snapshot values are frozen at queue time)
- Create an unpredictable testing surface

### Key Insight: Snapshots Protect In-Flight Reactions

The `activeQueue` system uses a `snapshot` field that freezes the reaction's `energyCost`, `reactants`, `productQuantity`, and other values at queue time. This means seed changes do **not** affect reactions that are already queued. Only new queue entries use updated values. Migration risk for in-flight reactions is therefore low.

### Approach: Incremental Per-Generation Passes

Do not change all four generations at once. Apply changes generation by generation, validate, then move on.

**Pass order:**

| Pass | Scope | Risk level | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q-B** | Gen 1 quantity | Very low | Small quantities, instant reactions, no serialized chain depth |
| **Q-C** | Gen 2 quantity + energy cost audit | Low | 8s–30s reactions; players unlikely to have deep Gen 2 saves |
| **Q-D** | Gen 3 quantity + energy cost | Medium | 60s–180s reactions; affects Lithium Ion Cell path |
| **Q-E** | Gen 4 quantity + energy cost | Medium | 180s–900s reactions; most time-sensitive for existing saves |
| **Q-F** | Energy income constants | Medium | Global constants; affects all players simultaneously |
| **Q-G** | Shard formula recalibration | Low-medium | Pure formula change; no inventory or queue impact |

**Principle: each pass should be independently deployable and testable.**

A Gen 1 quantity pass can go to production while Gen 2 is still in testing. Do not bundle multiple generations into a single deployment.

### What NOT to do

- Do not change `reactionTime` for reactions that are currently in active queue. The snapshot protects the player's expectation. If you extend a reaction from 90s to 180s, existing queued entries still resolve in 90s.
- Do not change `energyCost` for reactions mid-queue (snapshots handle this, but it's still confusing to change a seed while a player is waiting for it).
- Do not reseed the database for existing deployments. Modify seeds and write a targeted migration script if schema fields change. For value-only changes (`energyCost`, `quantity`, `reactionTime`), a direct MongoDB update or re-seed after collection drop is fine — but only in dev/staging first.

### Existing User Save Compatibility

| User data | Impact of seed changes | Migration needed |
|---|---|---|
| `user.inventory` | None. Inventory holds products, not recipe requirements. | No |
| `user.activeQueue` | Protected by snapshot. Existing queued reactions unaffected. | No |
| `user.runTotals` | Shard values per substance. If `shardValue` changes in seeds, existing `runTotals` use the old value at Big Bang time — value is read from substance at calculation time, not stored in runTotals. | Audit |
| `user.genesisShards` | Already accumulated. Changing the formula only affects future Big Bangs. | No |
| `user.unlockTier` | Unchanged. | No |

**One exception:** `calculateGenesisShards` reads `entry.substance.shardValue` at Big Bang time by populating `runTotals.substance`. If `shardValue` changes in the substance document, the next Big Bang will use the new value — even for substances the player produced before the change. This is acceptable behavior, but communicate it clearly in the migration notes.

## Part 6 — Prestige Integration

### Dependency Direction

Phase Q must be completed before the prestige economy is meaningful. The reason:

> Prestige pressure (the "I should Big Bang" signal) requires that the player encounters a wall they cannot overcome in the current run. If no wall exists economically, Big Bang provides no economic relief. Prestige upgrades have no meaningful value if they solve problems that don't exist.

Phase Q creates the walls. Prestige removes them (selectively, at cost). This ordering is mandatory.

### Genesis Shards

The shard formula (`unlockTier² - 1 + Σ(shardValue + log₂(produced+1))`) uses two inputs:
- `unlockTier` at Big Bang time — unchanged by Phase Q
- `shardValue` per substance × production volume — affected by quantity changes

If Gen 3–4 substances now require more intermediate units (quantity scarcity), players will produce fewer end-products per run but spend more time waiting. The shard payout for a given run depth may decrease (fewer product units produced) while investment time increases. This is the correct pressure: less return for the same effort creates the Big Bang impulse.

**Required recalibration:** After Q-B through Q-E, simulate or playtest a Gen 3 and Gen 4 run to measure actual shard payout. Adjust `shardValue` in substance seeds and/or the formula constants to ensure:
- A Gen 1-only run pays out 2–5 shards (barely worth it)
- A Gen 3 run pays out 15–30 shards (meaningful investment)
- A Gen 4 deep run pays out 40–80 shards (substantial reward)

These are structural targets, not final values. The formula recalibration pass (Q-G) handles this.

### Big Bang Route

`POST /api/bigbang` calls `calculateGenesisShards` and then resets the run. No changes to this route are needed in Phase Q. The output of the formula changes (because underlying substance production will change), but the route itself is correct.

### Blueprint Infrastructure

Blueprints (Phase P-A) survive Big Bang. Blueprint costs are currently 1 shard (placeholder). Phase Q does not set final blueprint costs — that is Phase R's job. But Phase Q's shard payout recalibration establishes the range within which blueprint costs must be designed. If a Gen 4 deep run yields 40–80 shards, and blueprints cost 1 each, they are trivially affordable. Blueprint costs must be set after Phase Q completes.

**Dependency:** Blueprint final costs (Phase R) depend on Phase Q shard payout calibration.

### Prestige Upgrade Recalibration

The existing `prestigeUpgrades.chemistry` multiplier reduces reaction energy costs by 5% per level. Currently this solves a non-problem. After Phase Q raises reaction energy costs:

- Chemistry multiplier becomes meaningful (saves real energy at higher levels)
- Matter multiplier (atom cost reduction) may need review — atoms are still free, so this remains mostly decorative
- Energy income multiplier (1 + 0.2 × level) will have more impact if energy income is reduced

The prestige upgrade system itself does not change in Phase Q. The values it operates on change, which shifts its effective power automatically.

## Part 8 — Risk Analysis

### Technical Risks

**Risk T1: Energy constant changes affect all users simultaneously.**
`ENERGY_MULTIPLIER` and `CLICK_ACTIVITY_GAIN` are global constants in `reactorRuntime.js`. There is no per-user flag. A change to these constants affects every connected session immediately on deploy. If the change makes the game feel worse, rollback requires a code deploy.
- Mitigation: Treat Q-E (energy rebalancing) as the riskiest deploy. Quantify the before/after energy income ratio before shipping. Keep changes minimal if Q-B through Q-D achieve the goal.

**Risk T2: Shard formula uses populated runTotals at Big Bang time.**
`calculateGenesisShards` populates `runTotals.substance` at call time. If a substance's `shardValue` changes in the database, the change applies to all pending Big Bangs immediately — including players mid-run who accumulated production before the change.
- Mitigation: Change `shardValue` seeds only in Q-F (last), when run dynamics are fully understood. Communicate the change as intentional rebalancing.

**Risk T3: Inventory quantity check in eligibility logic.**
`checkReactionEligibility` compares `user.inventory[sub].quantity >= reactant.quantity`. If a reaction's quantity requirement increases from 1 to 3, any user with fewer than 3 units in inventory will be blocked from queuing that reaction. This is correct behavior — but it may surprise players who previously queued freely.
- Mitigation: Accept this. It is the intended effect of scarcity. No code change needed.

---

### Balance Risks

**Risk B1: Quantity increases create exponential time walls in Gen 3–4 chains.**
If Graphene now requires Carbon Nanotube×3 as an input, and Carbon Nanotube is itself a 60-minute chain, a single Gen 4 capstone could require 5+ hours of serial time. This breaks the game for casual players.
- Mitigation: Quantity changes must be calibrated against chain depth. Deep chains get lower quantity multipliers than shallow chains. The IV audit script should flag when a proposed change would push the minimum serial synthesis time past a threshold.

**Risk B2: Shard payout drops to near-zero if production volume falls.**
If players produce fewer units of high-value substances because those substances require more intermediate stockpile, `log₂(produced+1)` terms decrease. A run that previously produced 5 Reactive Plasma Cores might now produce 1. The shard formula may need adjustment to weight depth of progress rather than production volume.
- Mitigation: Q-F handles this. The formula recalibration pass exists specifically to address this.

**Risk B3: Energy rebalancing makes early runs frustrating.**
If ENERGY_MULTIPLIER is reduced, Gen 1 reactions that currently feel instant (energyCost=1–8 with 40 energy/sec income) will require slightly more clicking. Gen 1 should never feel like a bottleneck.
- Mitigation: Any energy constant reduction should be verified to leave Gen 1 and Gen 2 feeling fluid. Gen 1 energy costs are 1–10; even with a 5× energy income reduction (to ~8/sec), these are cleared in under 2 seconds of clicking. Acceptable.

---

### Migration Risks

**Risk M1: Existing in-progress Gen 4 queue entries.**
Players mid-run with a 900-second reaction queued will see the seed change take effect for the next reaction they queue, but not the current one. This is correct behavior (snapshot protection). The risk is perception: players may notice that their next queue attempt requires more materials than before.
- Mitigation: Accept. This is the intended effect. No silent failures.

**Risk M2: Reseeding in staging vs. production divergence.**
If the staging database is reseeded (collection drop + reseed) while production uses a migration script to update specific fields, the two environments may have different substance/reaction ObjectIds. Routes that rely on ObjectId references (inventory, runTotals, activeQueue snapshots) could behave differently.
- Mitigation: Use consistent reseeding approach across all environments. In production, perform field-level updates (`db.reactions.updateOne({reactionKey: 'gen3_graphene'}, {$set: {energyCost: 80}})`) rather than dropping collections if there are active users.

---

### Save Compatibility Risks

**Risk S1: `runTotals` references substance ObjectIds.**
If substances are dropped and reseeded, ObjectIds change. Existing `runTotals` will have stale ObjectId references that no longer resolve. `calculateGenesisShards` will then get null on `entry.substance.shardValue` for those entries and skip them silently.
- Mitigation: Never drop the substance collection in production. Only update field values. If a substance is added, insert it. Never remove or re-create existing substances.

---

### UI Risks

**Risk U1: Inventory display doesn't signal stockpile requirements.**
The current inventory UI shows quantities but doesn't indicate how many are needed for a queued synthesis. If a reaction now requires Graphene×3, the player may not realize they need to produce more before queuing.
- Mitigation: The `SelectedReactionPanel` already shows reactant requirements. If it shows the required quantity from the reaction's `reactants[].quantity`, the player can see the gap between inventory and requirement. Verify this displays correctly after seed changes. Minor UI tweak may be needed.

**Risk U2: No player communication of balance changes.**
Players who return after a deploy may find their familiar recipes now require more materials. Without in-game communication, this feels like a bug.
- Mitigation: Out of scope for Phase Q implementation, but should be flagged. Consider a "lab conditions have changed" toast or session note for the first load after a balance deploy.

---

### Testing Risks

**Risk X1: No automated balance test suite.**
Seed data has no automated tests. A bad quantity value or energy cost is only caught by playtesting or manual audit.
- Mitigation: The IV audit script from Q-A serves as the primary verification tool. Run it before and after every seed change in Q-B through Q-E. Treat any unexpected IV anomaly as a blocking issue.

**Risk X2: Playtest coverage is limited to designer paths.**
Balance testing requires testing both expert paths (optimal queuing order) and naive paths (random queuing). The time wall is only visible if you test how long a random Gen 4 run actually takes.
- Mitigation: Define two test scenarios for each pass: (a) optimal route, (b) naive route. Measure completion time for both. If naive route is unreasonably long, quantity requirements are too high.
